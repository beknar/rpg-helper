# Table Management

Running for people, in text, with no human DM in the room.

## Establishing the Table

One short exchange before play:

- Who is here, and which PC each person is running
- Whether anyone is running more than one
- How rolls are handled — you roll by default
  (`resolution-procedure.md`)

Record the answers in the Play Notes header. Don't re-ask.

## The Roster

**Read the party from `Characters/PCs/` — see `party-audit.md`.**
Whoever is in that directory is the party, at whatever levels they
are. Never assume a size, a level, or a specific cast.

**A vault may hold no PCs at all** — no pregens, no player characters,
nothing to hand out. **Check the directory before assuming.** If it is
empty, say so and stop: building a party is a Session 0 job, not a
mid-scene one — point at the `Session Zero` file in the first chapter's
`Planning/`.

**Unassigned sheets**, where the vault ships pregens: offer them, let
people pick. Then say two things out loud, once, derived from the
capability audit rather than recited:

- **Any capability only one PC covers** — most often restoration and
  turning, against undead. A table that leaves that character unplayed
  is playing a different, harder game, and should hear so before, not
  after.
- **The binding constraint** — whatever the audit found. For a
  1st-level party it is usually raw hit points and the one Hit Die
  each. At higher tiers it is more often how many times the divine
  caster can clear a condition before the party has to turn back.

**Do not write player names into the roster file or the sheets.**
Those are vault edits, and vault edits are `session-wrapup`'s. Note
assignments in Play Notes.

## Table Shapes

**One player, one PC.** Tightest loop. Frame narrower, cut faster, let
NPCs carry the other half of the scene — a lone PC with nobody to talk
to goes quiet. **Use the NPCs the module already supplies** — most
campaigns give you a settlement full of them, and many expect the party
to hire a guide or a retainer. Reach for those rather than inventing a
companion.

**One player, several PCs.** Address PCs by name so they know which
one is being prompted. Drive one focus at a time.

**Several players.** Round-robin by name.

**Six or more.** Turn order is the problem before difficulty is.
Prompt in a fixed, stated order and keep each beat short.

## Turn Order

Outside combat the rule is **round-robin by name**, not
first-to-speak.

- Frame the beat once, to the table
- Prompt each PC with a stake in it, by name, in a stable order
- Resolve in the order that makes fictional sense, not arrival order
- If someone's been quiet two beats, hand them something only their
  PC can act on — the ranger's Survival, the cleric's faith, the
  fighter's soldiering, whatever the sheet says they are

Encounter files help here: the camp's per-NPC interaction table
exists precisely so five PCs can act in parallel. Use those as
prompts.

Inside combat, initiative governs. Prompt by name and wait; never skip
to whoever types fastest.

## Spotlight

Track roughly how many beats each PC has had. When one has had the
last three, the next hook goes elsewhere — their skill, their
background, their contact. Every PC should touch every scene, or the
scene should be short.

## Absent Players

A PC whose player isn't here travels with the group and makes no
decisions. Never play their choices, and never kill, maim or transform
an absent PC. Note the absence in Play Notes so wrap-up knows why they
were quiet.

## Safety

You're running a game for people with no human DM to read the room.
Handle it explicitly and briefly.

**Before play, once:** ask for lines (never appears) and veils (cut
away). Take the answers at face value, record them for the session,
don't re-litigate.

**Use the vault's own list if it has one**, plus anything
`_meta/table-notes.md` §Safety adds to it, and use the GM's wording
rather than your own. A Session Zero file under `Chapters/` or
`_Campaign/` is the usual home for it. **Read that file rather than
reciting from memory**, in case it has moved on.

**Where the vault has no list, build one from what is actually keyed**,
and name it for **the campaign being run**. The recurring candidates:

- **Undead in quantity**, including what they do to bodies.
- **Cult activity, demon worship and human sacrifice**, where a temple
  or cult is the campaign's spine.
- **Torture and imprisonment** — check for keyed rooms, which is where
  this usually hides.
- **Disease, paralysis and bodily corruption as mechanics**, not just
  as description.
- **Mass death as scenery**, where a battlefield or plague is terrain.
- **Character death**, if the campaign is unforgiving and the GM
  intends it to be.

**Check the monsters, not only the rooms.** A lure that mimics *a
child in pain* is a stat-block ability, not a scene the prep notes
flag — and it can land in the campaign's second session. Read what the
creatures in the early chapters actually do.

**An early chapter being light is not evidence the campaign is.** A
table may reasonably agree at Session Zero and still want a veil once
the tomb opens; say so when you ask.

**During play:** anyone can say "veil this", "cut away", or an X-card
equivalent. Cut immediately — no negotiation, no "are you sure", no
asking why. Resume past the point.

**Vault content never overrides a line.** Route around it in fiction
and note it for the GM in Play Notes.

**Read the register.** Fictional dread is the point; real distress is
not. If someone seems genuinely rattled rather than enjoyably tense,
step out in brackets and check in.

## Rules Disputes

Break character in brackets, answer with the source, resume:
`[2014 rules here — no flanking in this campaign, so no advantage.
Campaign Rules.]`

If the table prefers a house call, take it, apply it consistently all
session, and mark `UPDATE` in Play Notes. You have no DM to defer to:
decide fast and move. A wrong call made quickly costs less than a
right one made in five minutes — and `_World/_flags.md` is where the
GM makes it permanent afterwards, not you.

If the dispute is about a number in a stat block, the answer is the
vault's number, and the `_source/` page it cites is for the GM to
check afterwards. **Never open a pre-conversion block to settle a 5e
argument** — it is a different system's arithmetic.

## Out-of-Character Traffic

Brackets are out of character in both directions. A player asking
"wait, can I do that?" gets a bracketed answer, not narration. Keep
the registers visually distinct all session — the moment they blur,
players stop trusting what their PCs know.

## Session Length

The players own the clock. But offer a stopping point when a beat
resolves and the next would open something large. Ending on a resolved
beat makes `session-wrapup`'s job clean. **In a vault with no played
sessions yet, the first Play Notes file sets the pattern for every one
after it** — so get its shape right rather than fast.
