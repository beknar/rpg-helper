# Dialogue

Running conversation in both directions — NPCs speaking to players,
and eliciting speech from PCs. Read before any scene with a speaking
NPC, and whenever a social encounter is the encounter.

Campaigns deliver their clues through people. At low tier it is
Alder at the Cracked Tankard, a keyed village's named residents and the
rumour table; at higher tier a whole settlement is a social encounter with a dozen
fights folded into it, and the waste's guides, hermits and
peddlers are the module's clue delivery system. Conversation is the
mechanism, not the connective tissue.

## Reading an NPC for Conversation

The vault's NPC sections carry a consistent structure. Take all of it
before the first line:

| Field | What it governs |
|---|---|
| **Wants** | Every line they speak. State it to yourself in one clause |
| **Doing** | Their body — where they stand, what's in their hands |
| **Price** | What they will trade, and will name themselves if the party stalls |
| **Lines** | What they will not do for anything. These do not bend to a roll |
| **Free vs earned** | What they say if simply asked, versus what an act buys |
| **Disposition ladder** | Their attitude, and what moves it |

An NPC without these in the vault gets a want, a refusal, and one
verbal habit, invented on the spot and held for the session.

## The Disposition Ladder

Where an encounter gives one, it is **tracked state**, like HP.

- **Start where the file says.** Varn starts at Wary.
- **Move one step per genuine act**, in the direction the act earns.
  Acts, not words alone, and not rolls.
- **Steps can go down.** Drawing on someone undoes ninety seconds of
  goodwill. Paying in gold instead of camp scrip, in the camp, undoes
  more.
- **Show the step in behaviour before you name it.** He answers a
  second question without being pushed; she puts the knife back under
  the counter. The player should feel the ladder move without being
  told a number.
- **Put it in the state block** when it changes:

```text
Varn — Bargaining (was Wary; the party paid in scrip, up front)
```

Where no ladder exists, run the same logic informally: an NPC's
attitude is a fact about the scene that the party can change.

## Rolls Do Not Buy What Acts Buy

This is the rule most likely to be got wrong, and the vault states it
explicitly: *do not gate any of this behind a Persuasion check.*

- **Free information is free.** If the file says an NPC gives
  something when asked directly, they give it when asked directly.
  No roll. Rolling for it teaches the table that talking is a tax.
- **Earned information is earned by acts.** A step on the ladder is
  bought with a lowered weapon, a drink paid for, a wound healed —
  never with a d20.
- **A Line is a Line.** No roll crosses it. The Usurer will not take
  gold, and a natural 20 on Persuasion does not change that; it
  changes how kindly he refuses.
- **Never let a roll overwrite a motivation.** An NPC persuaded out
  of what they want is an NPC you have stopped playing.

### When a social roll *is* right

Call one when a **specific attempt** has an uncertain outcome and a
real cost to failing:

- Passing off a lie the NPC has reason to doubt → **Deception**
- Reading whether someone is holding back → **Insight**
- Pushing someone to act against mild self-interest → **Persuasion**
- Making someone believe a threat you may not mean → **Intimidation**

State the DC and the stakes first, as with any roll
(`resolution-procedure.md`). Failure changes the conversation — it
does not end it, and it does not mean the NPC becomes hostile by
default.

**Insight is not a lie detector.** It returns a read on behaviour:
*he's telling the truth about the road and leaving something out
about who else is on it.* Never hand over the NPC's private facts,
and never anything fenced (`canon-boundaries.md`).

## Speaking as an NPC

**Give the line, then the read.**

> "Never been past the barrows myself." *He says it to the door, not
> to you.*

The line is what they said; the read is what the PCs can observe.
Players deduce intent from behaviour — never state what an NPC feels
or intends.

**One verbal habit each, and no more.** Bjorc answers with a price.
Father Death never uses a name. Three tics is a cartoon.

**Vary line length by character and pressure.** A frightened person
is short. A person who has rehearsed a speech overruns it and knows
they're overrunning it.

**Written read-aloud goes where the file attaches it** — unpadded,
unparaphrased, and only when its moment arrives
(`narration-craft.md` §Using Written Read-Aloud).

**Let them not answer.** Silence, deflection, and "I'm not going to
be the one that tells you" are all legitimate turns.

## Several NPCs in One Scene

The Camp has a dozen people who can be approached, and the vault's
Camp encounter carries an interaction table saying what each gives.
That pattern is common here.

- **Attribute every line.** Name or unmistakable description before
  or inside the line, every time. Never a wall of unattributed
  quotes.
- **One voice at a time.** Two NPCs may interrupt each other once,
  for effect. Three is noise.
- **Run the interaction window as a window**, not a queue: frame that
  the party can mill, then let each PC approach whoever they choose,
  and give each approach its own short exchange.
- **NPCs react to each other.** Kess going quiet when Varn
  names a route is a free clue, and it happens because Kess is
  present, not because the table asked.

## Prompting PCs for Dialogue

Asking "what do you do?" gets actions. Getting *speech* needs a
different handle.

**Give them something to answer.** A question aimed at a named PC is
the cleanest prompt:

> *"'And who's paying for the guide?' He's looking at the one in the
> best armour when he says it. — she's waiting."*

**Name the PC.** In a group, an unaddressed question gets answered by
whoever types fastest, which is not the same as whoever should.

**Accept both registers, always:**

- **Direct speech** — *"I say: we pay in scrip, and we pay tonight."*
- **Stated intent** — *"I try to reassure him we're not here for the
  Usurer."*

Both are complete turns. Many players never voice their characters
aloud and should never be nudged to; a table where one player quotes
and another narrates is normal.

**Never convert intent into a quote.** If a player says "I try to
reassure him," you narrate that the fighter says something reassuring
and give Varn's response — you do not put words in the fighter's
mouth and present them as spoken. Inventing a PC's dialogue is the
same error as deciding their action.

**Ask for specifics only when the specifics matter.** If the
difference between "I lie" and the actual lie changes what the NPC
does, ask what they say. Otherwise take the intent and move.

**Don't demand performance for mechanical benefit.** A player who
narrates gets the same DC as a player who improvises a monologue.

## Sustaining a Conversation

- **Every NPC turn should give the party something to act on** — a
  fact, a refusal, a question, or a change in the room. A line that
  advances nothing is a line to cut.
- **Track what's been asked.** Don't re-offer information already
  given; do let an NPC repeat themselves when a player has genuinely
  missed it.
- **Escalate on stalling.** Where a file names what the NPC does if
  the party stalls — Varn naming his own price — use it rather
  than waiting.
- **Close windows in the fiction.** Sundown closes the camp; the file
  says nobody is out after dark, and it says why. Prefer that to
  announcing that the conversation is over.
- **Let conversations end badly.** A refusal the party can't move is
  a real outcome, and the encounter's Outcomes section will have a
  branch for it.

## Out-of-Character Traffic

Brackets are out of character in both directions
(`table-management.md`). A player asking "would he know that?" gets
a bracketed answer, not an in-fiction one. Keep the registers
visually distinct — in a dialogue-heavy scene this is what stops
players confusing what they know with what their PCs know.

## Play Notes

Conversation generates canon faster than combat does. Between beats:

- `NEW-NPC` anyone who spoke and wasn't in the vault
- `UPDATE` when a disposition ends the scene somewhere other than it
  started, or when an NPC commits to something
- `NEW-EVENT` for a promise, a bargain, or a refusal that will matter
  later
- Record the **substance** of what was said, not a transcript
